# TestStation V1 — standards applicability and insulation-coordination basis

**Date:** 2026-11-18  
**Status:** STANDARDS-IDENTIFICATION HOLD — REVIEW ONLY  
**Parent documents:** M2.9-P, M2.9-Q, M2.9-R and M2.9-S  
**Approval:** No standard edition, insulation class, working-voltage rating, creepage/clearance or component is approved by this study.

## 1. Why the standards question must be resolved first

The TestStation V1 is a measurement/test platform containing channels potentially connected to 230/400 VAC. It is not automatically the same product as an EV supply equipment (EVSE) charger. Therefore, citing IEC 61851-1 alone does not establish the safety standard for this measurement instrument.

The standards route depends on the intended product boundary and use:
- Is this a standalone electrical test/measurement instrument?
- Is it integrated into an EVSE product and assessed as part of that equipment?
- Is it a manufacturing/service test fixture, with operator access, probes, terminals and installation category defined?
- Is it intended for professional laboratory/industrial use, fixed installation, or field use?

These facts must be frozen in the product requirements before component safety ratings are selected.

## 2. Standards candidates and their roles

| Standard | Relevance | Current treatment |
|---|---|---|
| IEC 61010-1:2010 + AMD1:2016 consolidated edition | General safety requirements for electrical test/measurement, industrial process-control and laboratory equipment | Primary candidate if TestStation is standalone measurement/test equipment |
| IEC 61010-2-030 | Particular requirements for equipment having testing or measuring circuits | Strong candidate to assess alongside Part 1 if the TestStation has measurement circuits connected to hazardous live circuits |
| IEC 61010-2-032 | Particular requirements for hand-held and hand-manipulated current sensors | Applicability depends on whether such sensors are included; do not assume it covers an embedded PCB sensor |
| IEC 60664-1:2020 + AMD1:2025 consolidated edition | Insulation coordination for equipment up to AC 1,000 V / DC 1,500 V; clearances, creepage and solid insulation | Candidate engineering basis for insulation coordination, subject to product standard requirements |
| IEC 61851-1:2017 (including published corrigendum) | General requirements for EV supply equipment for conductive charging | Relevant to the EVSE being tested / integrated charging equipment; not automatically the TestStation's own product safety standard |
| IEC 61851-21-2 | EMC requirements for off-board EV charging systems | Potentially relevant to EVSE product/system, not a substitute for measurement-instrument safety requirements |

Official IEC catalogue references:
- [IEC 61010-1:2010 + AMD1:2016 consolidated edition](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/59769)
- [IEC 61010-2-030](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/33314)

The catalogue entries establish scope and edition metadata only. They do not replace the normative text, national adoption, amendments/corrigenda, or a formal applicability assessment. Verify the latest adopted EN/IEC versions and applicable EU legislation before a conformity route is fixed.

## 3. Inputs required before calculating insulation

The following are unresolved design inputs, not values to guess:

1. Product classification and intended use environment.
2. Maximum rated voltage and measurement category at each terminal/channel (CAT I/II/III/IV only after a formal assessment).
3. Nominal and maximum continuous working voltage from each sensed node to protective earth, between phases, and across each isolation barrier.
4. Expected temporary overvoltage, impulse/surge level, transient waveform and source impedance.
5. Overvoltage category, pollution degree, material group / CTI, altitude and enclosure environment.
6. Basic, supplementary, double or reinforced insulation objective for each protective separation.
7. Required dielectric test, partial-discharge or impulse test conditions where applicable.
8. Component safety certification scope and exact conditions of recognition.
9. PCB creepage/clearance, slots, coatings, solder mask treatment, connector and wiring insulation, test pads, mounting hardware and enclosure barriers.
10. Fault model, protective earth, accessible conductive parts, operator access and single-fault safety state.

Do not assign CAT III, reinforced insulation, or a specific clearance based only on the fact that the system is nominally 400 VAC. The measurement category and impulse environment are determined by where and how the instrument is connected, not just its nominal voltage.

## 4. Consequence for isolated DC/DC selection

A DC/DC converter's dielectric withstand test voltage is not equivalent to its permitted continuous working voltage or an insulation system classification. The shortlist must require manufacturer evidence for the exact part number and package:

- Continuous working voltage across the relevant barrier (for example VIORM/VIOWM, where specified).
- Repetitive peak and transient/impulse voltage ratings.
- Basic/reinforced insulation classification and applicable standard editions.
- Certified creepage/clearance and insulation construction.
- Pollution degree, altitude, material group and any derating/conditions.
- Certification body, certificate/file number, recognized production series and exact suffix.
- Output-to-input capacitance and common-mode transient behaviour, in addition to electrical safety.
- Thermal and fault behaviour under the proposed input/output loading.

A component may be screened out if the manufacturer does not publish the necessary evidence. A 1 kV or 3 kV one-minute test figure alone cannot pass this gate.

## 5. MEE1S2405SC status carried forward

M2.9-S classified MEE1S2405SC as NOT APPROVED for mains-referenced floating high-side supplies because the evidence available did not establish the complete continuous working-voltage, surge, insulation-class and certification conditions required for this application.

M2.9-T does not upgrade or reverse that status. The converter remains on HOLD until the exact manufacturer safety file and the system insulation-coordination calculation are both available and reviewed. No substitute MPN is selected.

## 6. Proposed requirements freeze gate

Before an isolated-converter shortlist is published as a design recommendation, create and approve a product safety basis containing:
- Product boundary and intended use statement.
- Applicable standard(s), editions, amendments and national adoptions.
- Terminal/measurement category assignment with rationale.
- Voltage/transient/impulse table for each channel and isolation barrier.
- Pollution degree, material group and altitude assumptions.
- Required insulation type per barrier.
- Required component certification and PCB spacing rules.
- Applicable single-fault and accessibility requirements.

Only after that gate can the converter matrix compare candidates. Until then, the only valid output is a screening matrix with explicit unknowns and exclusions, not a selected component.
