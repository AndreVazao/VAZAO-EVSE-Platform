# TestStation V1 — Current-channel design envelope proposal (2026-11-13)

Status: **PROVISIONAL ENGINEERING DESIGN TARGET — NOT A FROZEN REQUIREMENT**
Scope: six TMCS1123 current channels, Zone A and Zone B
Parent evidence: `M2.9-K-CURRENT-CHANNEL-RANGE-AUDIT-2026-11-13.md`

## 1. Decision made for continued engineering

The project owner delegated the selection of a sensible engineering starting point because a waveform peak/overload specification was not previously available. The following values are therefore adopted as a **provisional design envelope for analysis**, not as a certified product specification:

| Parameter | Provisional target | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Continuous phase current | 32 A RMS | Existing M2.3 requirement |
| Nominal sine peak | 45.25 A | (32\sqrt{2}) |
| Measurement peak range | −70 A to +70 A | Target instantaneous linear measurement range |
| +25% overload scenario | 40 A RMS for 10 s | Engineering test scenario; thermal safety not established |
| +50% overload scenario | 48 A RMS for 1 s | Engineering test scenario; thermal safety not established |
| Fundamental frequency | 50/60 Hz | Existing project context |
| ADC PGA starting point | 1 | ADS131M06 differential FSR ±1.2 V nominal |

The 40 A/10 s and 48 A/1 s scenarios are proposed test points only. They do **not** define the TestStation's fault-current withstand, protective-device trip curve, safe operating area or permission to energize. Actual waveform crest factor, repetitive duty, ambient temperature and board thermal conditions remain to be specified and tested.

## 2. Why ±70 A peak

A 48 A RMS sinusoid has a peak of:

`48 × √2 = 67.88 A`

A ±70 A measurement target covers that scenario with a small measurement-range allowance. It is not a claim that all real fault transients are limited to 70 A. The system must separately define what happens when the waveform exceeds the measurement range, including hardware protection and data-quality flags.

## 3. Sensor candidate screening

| Variant | Linear sensor range | 32 A RMS sine | 40 A RMS sine | 48 A RMS sine | ±70 A peak target |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A3, 5 V | ±32 A | Fail | Fail | Fail | Fail |
| A2, 5 V | ±48 A | Pass, little margin | Fail | Fail | Fail |
| A1, 5 V | ±96 A | Pass | Pass | Pass electrically | Pass electrically |
| B1, 3.3 V | ±62 A | Pass | Pass, limited margin | Fail | Fail |

**Preferred candidate for the next analogue-front-end study: TMCS1123A1AQDVGR at 5 V.** It is the only currently compared variant with sufficient symmetric sensor linear range for the provisional ±70 A peak target. This is a candidate recommendation, not a BOM selection or purchasing authorization.

A1's sensor output is centred at 2.5 V and has 25 mV/A sensitivity. At ±70 A, the nominal output excursion is ±1.75 V, giving an approximate VOUT range of 0.75 V to 4.25 V. The sensor's own linear range is ±96 A, so ±70 A is inside its nominal linear range. Tolerances, temperature drift, saturation recovery, thermal SOA and transient behaviour still require manufacturer-data review and bench validation.

B1 cannot meet ±70 A because its linear range is ±62 A. The 48 A RMS scenario reaches 67.88 A peak and is outside that range.

## 4. ADS131M06 interface direction

A1 cannot be connected directly to the 3.3 V ADS131M06 inputs: its 2.5 V zero-current output plus/minus signal excursion can exceed the ADC's individual input-voltage limits. Also, A1's unscaled differential excursion at ±70 A is ±1.75 V, above the ADS131M06 PGA=1 differential FSR of ±1.2 V.

Initial analogue design target:

- Sensor supply: 5 V, with local decoupling and monitored rail.
- Convert the sensor's VOUT/VREF differential signal through a defined, high-input-impedance conditioning stage.
- Differential attenuation target: approximately 0.60, giving about ±1.05 V at ±70 A.
- Drive the ADC with a controlled common-mode near 1.65 V, so the two ADC inputs remain nominally centred within the 3.3 V analogue domain.
- ADS131M06 PGA initially 1; retain margin for sensor sensitivity tolerance, reference error, amplifier offset, resistor tolerance and transient overshoot.
- Use a symmetric, fully analysed differential filter and ADC-compatible driver/source impedance.

At the nominal ±70 A target, the ideal attenuated differential magnitude is:

`70 A × 25 mV/A × 0.60 = 1.05 V`

This is only an initial transfer target. The 0.60 factor is not yet a resistor ratio or approved circuit. It must be recalculated with worst-case tolerances and verified against both individual AIN pin limits and differential full-scale. Do not implement by connecting A1 VOUT or VREF directly to an AIN pin.

## 5. Thermal and protection gate

The electrical linear-range comparison does not establish that the primary conductor, package, PCB copper, terminals or enclosure can safely carry 40 A RMS for 10 s or 48 A RMS for 1 s. TI's continuous-current ratings are temperature-dependent and do not by themselves establish pulse duration/repetition capability in this PCB.

Before any overload test or energized prototype, obtain/derive:
- applicable TI transient and thermal safe-operating-area limits for the exact A1 orderable;
- actual ambient and initial device/PCB temperature;
- primary conductor and terminal resistance/tolerance;
- PCB copper thickness, width, layer transitions and thermal path;
- repetition interval and worst-case duty cycle;
- protection device and disconnection time;
- enclosure airflow and adjacent heat sources.

Until then, the overload rows are **analysis scenarios only** and must not be used as permission to apply those currents.

## 6. Requirements still open

1. Confirm whether ±70 A is adequate for the actual waveform crest factor and fault/transient envelope.
2. Define maximum overload repetition and recovery time.
3. Define ambient temperature and maximum PCB/component temperatures.
4. Complete the A1-to-ADS131M06 circuit, including common-mode, absolute maximum, input impedance, anti-alias response, phase matching, noise and fault behaviour.
5. Check six-channel simultaneous operation, sensor supply budget, reference distribution and calibration.
6. Define over-range detection and a safe measurement-invalid state.
