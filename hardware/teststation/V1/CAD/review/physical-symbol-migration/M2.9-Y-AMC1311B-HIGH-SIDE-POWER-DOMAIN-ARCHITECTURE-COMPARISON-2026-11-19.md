# M2.9-Y — AMC1311B High-Side Power-Domain Architecture Comparison

**Date:** 2026-11-19  
**Status:** ARCHITECTURE STUDY — NOT APPROVED FOR IMPLEMENTATION  
**Scope:** Six AMC1311B measurement channels U3–U8 in TestStation V1  
**Relationship:** Companion to M2.9-X isolation-crossing inventory in PR #9

## 1. Purpose and terminology

Compare three candidate arrangements for powering the floating/high-side domains of six AMC1311B channels. This comparison is qualitative and does not establish electrical compatibility, isolation compliance, safety, or component selection.

Keep these counts distinct:
- **Measurement channels:** six AMC1311B instances.
- **Signal barriers:** six device-level barriers, subject to actual wiring and system-level review.
- **Power islands:** could be six, grouped, or a different number; not decided.
- **Isolation crossings:** include signal and power paths and every external/interface bypass.

## 2. Candidate A — Six independent high-side power islands

One isolated power source per AMC1311B high-side domain.

**Potential advantages**
- Strongest candidate for channel-to-channel fault containment and independent floating references.
- Avoids intentionally sharing high-side supply returns between channels.
- Easier conceptual mapping from channel to power island and fault analysis.

**Potential disadvantages**
- Highest converter count, cost, board area and component inventory.
- More converter-to-converter variation, EMI sources, startup sequencing interactions and failure points.
- Requires careful layout and verification of each barrier and all interface crossings.

**Open risks**
- Independence is not achieved if sensor wiring, shields, connectors, PE/chassis, communications or other circuitry reconnect domains.
- Six converters do not automatically mean six compliant isolation barriers. Ratings and conditions must be assessed for the complete system.

## 3. Candidate B — Grouped high-side domains (e.g. two groups of three)

Two isolated supplies feed groups of three channels only as a comparison example; no group assignment is proposed.

**Potential advantages**
- Lower converter count and possible reduction in area/cost.
- May be feasible if the channels in each group are proven to share an electrically compatible reference and measurement environment.

**Potential disadvantages**
- A shared high-side reference couples channels within each group.
- A short, wiring error, sensor fault or domain fault may affect multiple channels.
- Grouping can create unintended measurement return paths, alter common-mode conditions, or affect channel-to-channel isolation expectations.
- Requires a clear channel grouping and common-mode/fault analysis.

**Gate:** Do not group channels unless their intended sensor connections, voltage relationships, measurement category/transient environment, common-mode range, return paths and required fault containment are documented and reviewed. A grouped domain may be incompatible with the intended phase/line measurement topology.

## 4. Candidate C — One common isolated high-side domain

A single isolated source supplies all six high-side circuits.

**Potential advantages**
- Lowest isolated-converter count among these three candidates.
- Potentially simplest power distribution and lowest component cost.

**Potential disadvantages**
- All high-side references become coupled by the shared supply/return architecture.
- A common-domain fault may affect all channels.
- May defeat intended independent phase or sensor references and introduce conductive paths between measurement points.
- Isolation to the low-side remains only as strong as the complete shared power crossing and other paths; shared power does not itself provide channel isolation.

**Gate:** Treat as the least-separated candidate. Do not consider it acceptable without an explicit system topology proving compatibility, fault behaviour, measurement integrity, and standards-derived protection requirements.

## 5. Comparative matrix

| Criterion | A: six islands | B: grouped islands | C: common island |
|---|---|---|---|
| Converter count | 6 (candidate) | 2 (example only) | 1 (candidate) |
| Channel reference coupling | Lowest intentional coupling | Coupled within groups | Coupled across all six |
| Fault containment potential | Highest of these candidates | Intermediate, group dependent | Lowest of these candidates |
| Cost/area potential | Highest | Intermediate | Lowest |
| Architecture complexity | Many isolated supplies | Group design and grouping proof | Shared-domain compatibility proof |
| Channel independence | Best candidate | Partial, conditional | Not independent |
| System isolation compliance | Unproven | Unproven | Unproven |
| Approved/implemented | No | No | No |

These are qualitative tendencies, not safety rankings or design approval.

## 6. Required decisions before architecture selection

1. Confirm product boundary, intended use and applicable standard route/editions.
2. Define each of the six measurement channels: measured node(s), sensor/reference connection, expected voltage and polarity, operating/overvoltage/transient environment, and user-accessible interfaces.
3. Define required channel-to-channel isolation (if any), fault containment and measurement accuracy/integrity under shared references.
4. Derive continuous working voltage, impulse, insulation coordination, pollution degree, material group, altitude, creepage/clearance and component certification conditions.
5. Map every crossing and possible bypass: isolated converter, AMC signal barrier, sensor wiring, USB/debug, Ethernet, shields, connector shells, PE/chassis, auxiliary rails and PCB parasitics.
6. Compare candidate layouts, EMI, thermal, startup/brownout and single-fault behaviour.
7. Obtain qualified review before schematic or BOM implementation.

## 7. Decision status

**No architecture is selected.** Candidate A is the conservative reference architecture for evaluating independent channel fault containment, but it is not approved, and independence cannot be claimed until all signal, power, wiring and interface paths are mapped. Candidates B and C remain conditional study options and must not be implemented based on cost or converter-count assumptions alone.

## 8. Change boundary

Documentation-only. No production MASTER, BOM, netlist, pin-to-net mapping, PCB, firmware or test procedure changes. No converter part number, channel grouping, isolation class, CAT rating, system safety claim or clearance/creepage value is approved. No fabrication or energization is authorized.
