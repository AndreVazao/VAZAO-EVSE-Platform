## 11. High-side supply load-budget check — AMC1311B (TI Rev C)

TI AMC1311B Rev C (June 2022) specifies VDD1 = 3.0–5.5 V for AMC1311B. High-side supply current with SHTDN low: 6.0 mA typical / 8.4 mA maximum at 3.0–3.6 V; at 4.5–5.5 V, 7.1 mA typical / 9.7 mA maximum. (AMC1311 non-B values differ; this note is specifically AMC1311B.)

At a nominal 5 V high-side rail, AMC1311B alone is therefore approximately 35.5 mW typical and 48.5 mW at the published 9.7 mA maximum. Across six channels, this is approximately 213 mW typical / 291 mW maximum, before any external divider, filter, protection, indicator or additional circuitry. This is a datasheet arithmetic estimate only, not a full rail budget.

**Critical interaction with RECOM unregulated modules:** the 1 W RxxPxx/R datasheet says output specifications may not be met below 10% load. For 1 W/5 V/200 mA, 10% load is 20 mA. AMC1311B high-side alone draws only 7.1–9.7 mA at 4.5–5.5 V, i.e. below this 20 mA threshold. The 2 W/5 V/400 mA candidate's 10% threshold would be 40 mA, even farther above the AMC-only load. Thus neither 1 W nor 2 W unregulated candidate can be assumed to meet rail tolerance/ripple at the actual load. Additional loading or a regulated topology might be needed, but do not add a dummy load or choose another module until total load, efficiency/thermal, output tolerance, startup/fault and measurement error implications are evaluated.

This is a **material candidate-screening concern** and strengthens the no-selection decision. Required next: calculate per-channel complete high-side load and evaluate actual output behavior across input/load/temperature, including minimum-load strategy and power wasted in any load resistor, before continuing shortlist.

TI source: https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/amc1311.pdf (AMC1311x Rev C, June 2022, section 7.9). RECOM source: RxxPxx/R datasheet revision 6/2020; link retained in document.

## 14. Low-load mitigation analysis — RHV2 plus post-regulator / preload (2026-11-21)

This section evaluates topology consequences only. It does not authorize a circuit change or component selection.

### 14.1 A post-regulator does not remove the converter's minimum-load condition

An LDO or switching regulator connected to the RHV2 output draws only the current required by its downstream load plus its own quiescent current. The RHV2 therefore still sees a light load. A low-IQ post-regulator may make that load even smaller. It cannot be counted as a solution to the RHV2 datasheet warning that specifications may not be met below 10% load (40 mA for the 2 W / 5 V model).

A post-regulator could improve the downstream rail only if its input headroom, dropout, startup, stability, transient response and input-voltage range are all satisfied. RHV2 is unregulated and its published load regulation is ±15% maximum over 10–100%; the output must not be assumed to provide enough headroom for a 4.5–5.0 V regulated rail across input, load and temperature. The published ±5% output accuracy and ±15% load regulation must be interpreted using the manufacturer's exact test conditions; they must not be simplistically added or treated as a guaranteed combined worst-case without the full datasheet definitions.

### 14.2 Hypothetical preload arithmetic — not a design recommendation

To reach 40 mA at an assumed 5.0 V, a resistive preload alone would be 125 Ω and dissipate 0.20 W. If the converter output were 4.25 V, 125 Ω would draw only 34 mA and fail to reach the 40 mA threshold. A 100 Ω illustrative resistor would draw 42.5 mA at 4.25 V and dissipate 0.181 W; at 5.75 V it would draw 57.5 mA and dissipate 0.331 W.

Across six channels, that illustrative 100 Ω preload would dissipate approximately 1.09 W at 4.25 V per rail, 1.50 W at 5.0 V, or 1.99 W at 5.75 V, before converter losses and AMC1311B loads. These are arithmetic examples only. They do not establish that the RHV2 output actually remains within 4.25–5.75 V or that a 100 Ω preload guarantees compliance with the manufacturer's load test conditions. Resistor tolerance, temperature coefficient, power derating, enclosure temperature and fault behaviour are not included.

A preload is therefore a real thermal and efficiency penalty, not a free fix. It also does not improve output regulation accuracy; it only moves the operating point into a higher-load region.

### 14.3 Why a preload plus LDO remains unproven

A preload could keep the converter above its stated 10% load region while an LDO powers the AMC1311B. However:
- the LDO input must remain above its output by the required dropout at every allowed input/load/temperature corner;
- the unregulated RHV2 output and ±15% load-regulation figure make this headroom unproven;
- the preload must be sized against the minimum actual converter output voltage, not nominal 5 V;
- the preload and LDO input current add, and six channels multiply the heat;
- LDO quiescent current, output accuracy, noise/PSRR, startup, reverse current, short-circuit and thermal behaviour must be assessed;
- the AMC1311B VDD1 range is 3.0–5.5 V, but selecting a nominal 5 V rail still requires a complete tolerance and transient budget to avoid exceeding 5.5 V or dropping below the intended operating point;
- no divider, filter or protection load may be invented to make the arithmetic appear complete.

Consequently, RHV2 + preload + LDO is a possible investigation branch, not an accepted topology. It is not presently demonstrably safer, smaller, cooler or more reliable than selecting a regulated isolated converter with suitable safety evidence.

### 14.4 Alternative-converter search result

A further manufacturer-oriented search for a small regulated, no-minimum-load converter with explicit IEC/EN 61010-1 and reinforced insulation did not return a sufficiently evidenced exact low-power candidate to add to the shortlist. A Vicor 24 V-input Micro family document surfaced with regulated no-load operation and a 3 kVrms input-output test statement described as meeting reinforced-insulation requirements, but the surfaced evidence did not establish a suitable 5 V low-power model, IEC 61010-1 certification scope, or suitability for this TestStation. It is not added as a candidate.

This is not proof that no suitable product exists. The search should continue against exact manufacturer datasheets and certificate/CB reports, rather than promoting a family-level search result into a component recommendation.

### 14.5 Updated engineering conclusion

1. **No RHV2 post-regulator topology is approved.**
2. **No preload value is selected.** The 100 Ω example is only an explicit thermal illustration.
3. **No converter is selected.** RHV2 remains the strongest documentary candidate found so far, but its low-load behaviour and safety-critical-use authorization remain unresolved.
4. A regulated isolated converter with exact IEC 61010-1 certificate scope, reinforced insulation, adequate rated working voltage and documented no-minimum-load operation remains the preferred search direction, subject to system requirements.
5. The actual six channel power budgets cannot be completed until the exact divider/filter/protection topology and component values are recovered and the disconnected MASTER design is resolved through the normal native-KiCad workflow.
6. No MASTER, BOM, netlist, footprint, PCB, firmware, test procedure or safety classification changed. No fabrication, energization or certification is authorized.
