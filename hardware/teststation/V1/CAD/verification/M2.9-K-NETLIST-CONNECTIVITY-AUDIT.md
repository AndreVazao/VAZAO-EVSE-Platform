## Manufacturer datasheet cross-check — preliminary

Official manufacturer references consulted for this triage:

- Texas Instruments, ADS131M06 datasheet, SBAS949A: https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ads131m06.pdf
- Texas Instruments, TMCS1123 datasheet (current product page links the latest revision): https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tmcs1123.pdf
- Product family page for TMCS1123: https://www.ti.com/product/TMCS1123

The legacy cached symbols themselves reveal an important modelling defect:

- The cached `ADS131M06IPBSR` symbol declares only 12 pins (AIN0P/N through AIN2P/N plus DOUT, DIN, SCLK, CS, DRDY). The official ADS131M06 is a 32-pin device. Its documented pin functions also include AIN3–AIN5, AGND, AVDD, DGND, DVDD, CAP, REFIN, SYNC/RESET, XTAL1/CLKIN, and XTAL2, among others. The cached symbol therefore omits many real package pins and cannot serve as a complete symbol for pin-level validation.
- The cached `TMCS1123A3AQDVGR` symbol declares 8 pins. The official TMCS1123 DVG package is 10-pin and includes VOC (pin 9) and NC (pin 10) in addition to the functional pins. The cached symbol also declares pin 2 `IN-` as an electrical **output**, while TI describes IN− as an analog input. That incorrect electrical type can create a misleading ERC output-to-output conflict when this pin is connected to ADS131M06 DOUT.
- The above confirms that ERC messages must be interpreted in the context of verified symbols. It does **not** establish that the net connections are correct: the exported connectivity still ties TMCS1123 pin 3 GND to ADS131M06 pin 17 CS, and TMCS1123 pin 8 VS to ADS131M06 pin 19 SCLK. Those connections remain suspicious and must be traced in the schematic and verified against design intent.

This is a preliminary datasheet cross-check, not a complete component-library certification. Confirm the exact orderable part/package suffix and current datasheet revision for every BOM line before creating production symbols or footprints.

## Engineering interpretation

1. The present native schematic is **not electrically complete**. Only three multi-node nets are exported across 24 components.
2. The legacy schematic title comment “REAL ELECTRICAL CONNECTIVITY BASELINE” is not supported by the current exported connectivity evidence. The custom cached symbols are also incomplete/inaccurate: the ADS131M06 symbol omits many package pins, and the TMCS1123 symbol omits two package pins and misclassifies IN− as an output.
3. The existing ERC result is a useful diagnostic baseline, not a pass: 300 errors and 53 warnings remain.
4. Do not fabricate a PCB, order assembled hardware, or energize mains from this artifact.
5. Do not “fix” the result by adding blanket ERC exclusions, power flags, or arbitrary wires. Each change must follow verified pin-level design intent.

## Next steps — ordered, evidence-first

1. Preserve the legacy source and current generated artifacts as the baseline.
2. Preserve the eight custom cached symbols as historical evidence, then create corrected project-local KiCad symbols rather than mechanically converting incorrect pin definitions. Register them using a project-local symbol table.
3. Validate every symbol pin number, pin name, electrical type, unit, and orientation against the exact manufacturer datasheet. The ADS131M06 and TMCS1123 symbol defects above must be corrected and reviewed first. Record part number and datasheet revision/source for each component family.
4. Build a pin-to-net worksheet per functional block: isolated voltage sensing, isolated supplies, current sensing, ADCs, MCU/control, and communications. Mark intended connections as **unverified** until backed by the circuit design and datasheets.
5. Review the three nets above first, especially the output-to-output connection between U19 pin 2 and U2 pin 20.
6. Reconstruct intended wires and labels only from verified evidence. Keep unresolved nets explicitly unresolved.
7. Rerun ERC after each reviewed block. Classify each remaining finding as fixed, valid-by-design with evidence, or unresolved; do not bulk-suppress.
8. Run PCB DRC only after a PCB exists and its footprints, net assignments, clearances, creepage/clearance rules, and safety constraints have been reviewed.
9. Require qualified electrical/safety review before fabrication or mains energization.
